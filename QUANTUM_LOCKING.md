# Quantum Locking: Trapped Flux Coordinate Trajectory

This module outlines the mechanics required to lock a suspended object to the physical trajectory of the generator matrix, allowing a levitating mass to move synchronously with the contraption.

## 1. Flux Pinning Mechanics
By utilizing a Type-II YBCO Superconducting substrate within the base plate and embedding a high-remanence permanent magnet (NdFeB) within the target mass, the system transitions from standard levitation to true quantum locking. 

As the substrate cools below its critical temperature ($T_c$), the magnetic flux lines are squeezed into quantized strands known as Abrikosov vortices. Structural defects within the superconducting crystal lattice physically trap these vortices.

## 2. Geometric Coordinate Rigidness
This trapping forces a rigid coupling between the magnetic field of the suspended mass and the reference frame of the device. The spatial gap ($d$) between the plate and the mass becomes fixed. The system exhibits high restorative forces against any translational or rotational displacement:

* Linear Displacement: Pushing or pulling the mass triggers an instant quantum mechanical restoring force, behaving like a zero-mass structural bracket.
* Trajectory Synchronization: When the base contraption is accelerated through space along any kinetic axis, the pinned flux tubes drag the suspended mass along the identical vector, preserving the localized spatial configuration in open air.
