# Rotational Propulsion: Gravitomagnetic Metric Torsion

This module outlines the operational mechanics for altering gravity in open-space and atmospheric planes. By substituting the brute-force energy requirement of linear laser convergence with the high-velocity kinetic rotation of a coherent quantum state, we achieve localized metric manipulation without the need for static magnetic loops.

## 1. The Mechanic: Coherent Torsion
Instead of attempting to compress the grid lines vertically, the Rotational Propulsion Core uses a high-speed, spinning superconducting ring built into the vehicle's hull. 

When cooled to cryogenic temperatures, millions of electrons fuse into a singular, macro-sized quantum wave function (Cooper pairs). Spin-stabilizing this massive quantum state at thousands of RPMs acts like a mechanical stirrer spinning in water: it forces the local pathways of space to twist. In physics, this dragging of the spatial grid is known as Frame-Dragging or the Lense-Thirring Effect.

## 2. The Math: Frame-Dragging Scaling
In classical General Relativity, the gravitomagnetic field generated by a spinning mass is incredibly weak. The standard equation for frame-dragging torque is:

Omega = (2G * J) / (c^2 * r^3)

Where J is the angular momentum of the spinning mass. 

To make this practical for an atmospheric plane under modern technological constraints, QGT introduces a Quantum Coupling Coefficient (alpha). We hypothesize that when matter enters a perfectly coherent quantum state, its ability to grip and displace the universal surface tension increases exponentially.

By driving alpha upward via precise electromagnetic stimulation of the superconductor, the machine can twist the local metric coordinates using a fraction of the energy required by a linear squeeze.

## 3. Open-Air Advantages
Unlike magnetic levitation, which balloons out and fails in open skies, Metric Torsion alters the coordinate geometry of space itself. Because it modifies the universal tracks that matter moves through, the vehicle experiences the same gravity-altering effects whether it is hovering in Earth's lower atmosphere or navigating the absolute vacuum of deep space.
