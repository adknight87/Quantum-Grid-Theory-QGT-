# Thrust Modulation: Asymmetric Flux Vectoring

This module details how to convert a static Quantum Lock into an active directional propulsion system for atmospheric and space flight without the use of chemical propellants.

## 1. Asymmetric Field Distortion
To generate directional thrust, the rigid symmetry of the pinned flux tubes must be dynamically broken. The system achieves this by replacing the static magnetic matrix with a Phased Electromagnetic Core capable of micro-second vector adjustments.

By modulating the current feeding the directional coils, the system alters the spatial geometry of the magnetic mold. Pushing the field density forward forces the pinned Abrikosov vortices to migrate along the substrate lattice. The locked vehicle reacts to this internal displacement by accelerating toward the newly shifted magnetic equilibrium point.

## 2. Multi-Axis Maneuvering
Because the quantum lock controls all six degrees of freedom, modulating the current across different quadrants allows for total flight maneuvering:

* Forward/Backward Propulsion: Linear phase shifts along the longitudinal axis of the plate grid.
* Lateral Strafing: Anisotropic field adjustments along the transverse axis.
* Pitch, Roll, and Yaw: Differential vector scaling across the outer quadrant rings, tilting the aircraft by warping the local coordinate attachment points.

## 3. Propellantless Operation
Because the mechanical work is performed by the elastic restoration forces of the pinned quantum field against the metric of space, the vehicle requires zero onboard fuel or mass expulsion to alter its trajectory. The drive operates purely on electrical power fed to the cryogenic stator arrays.
